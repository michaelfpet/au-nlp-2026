## **Student Asked Questions**

### **1\. How should I prepare my cheat sheet?**

The design of your cheat sheet is a personal choice and should reflect your learning style and what you find useful to have available during the exam.

As general guidance, consider including key definitions, formulas, distinctions between related concepts, and brief examples or reminders for topics you find difficult to remember. Organize the material clearly so that you can find information quickly.

The exam will primarily assess conceptual understanding and your ability to apply course concepts. However, you will still need to know important facts and definitions; you should not assume that all relevant information will be provided. Your cheat sheet can help you keep track of these details.

### **2\. Will the exam include questions about understanding, debugging, or writing code?**

**Yes.**

These questions will assess your understanding of how course concepts are expressed in code. For example, you may be asked to determine what a code snippet does, identify a bug, or select code that implements a given operation. Within the exam’s multiple-choice format, you will select an answer rather than submit a standalone program.

When preparing, practise reading code and reasoning about its behaviour, including how changes to the code affect the result. To help you understand the nature of these questions and practice them better, we have updated the mock exam.

### **3\. Will material from Week 11, the week of the exam, be included?**

**No.** Material introduced in Week 11 will not be assessed in this exam. To clarify, all the content taught from **Week 1 (starting 23rd August)** through **Week 10 (ending 30th October)** will be included in the exam. The content discussed during the week of the exam won’t be included.
